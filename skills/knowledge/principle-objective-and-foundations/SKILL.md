# Objective and foundations

Plan backward from an observable outcome, then teach forward through its necessary foundations.

**Why:** A narrow goal can still hide substantial prerequisites. A useful map exposes those steps without treating every related concept as required or asking learners to describe their own knowledge state.

**Apply:**

- Define what the learner should explain, predict, interpret, or do, at a stated depth. The same topic needs different foundations for conceptual explanation, derivation, or implementation.
- Assume everyday language and basic arithmetic unless explicitly changed. Teach needed domain basics in ordinary opening sections, which learners may mark complete and reopen. Completion does not establish mastery or justify removing prerequisites from the canonical graph.
- Represent small observable capability types; author particular questions and answers later. Keep each item useful for instruction and practice, rather than splitting every term into a separate node.
- Require a concrete reason for each prerequisite in the selected route. Distinguish helpful context and alternative approaches. Coordinate relevant branches without expanding unrelated background.
- Ground capability content in inspected sources and label inferred dependencies. Research missing foundations recursively; stop at a justified baseline or an explicit scope split, with remaining gaps visible.
- Preserve the graph before lesson authoring. Keep instructional dependencies separate from causal or conceptual relationships, which may have different directions or cycles.

**Check:** Trace the target action to its entry capabilities. Can those be taught from the declared baseline? Does every retained item contribute to the objective, and are uncertain dependencies visible? Do not infer a learner's knowledge state from this instructional plan.
