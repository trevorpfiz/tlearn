# Source integrity

Keep every teaching claim connected to what was actually inspected, and distinguish evidence from interpretation.

**Why:** A citation can exist without supporting a claim. A fluent summary can lose conditions or uncertainty. Reusable notes help only when another agent can recover the underlying evidence.

**Apply:**

- Check the relevant passage before treating a source as support. Mark title-only, abstract-only, inaccessible, or incomplete reads explicitly; never present discovery as inspection.
- Retain stable source IDs, canonical links, versions, inspected locations, and the scope of each note. Cite the original page, section, figure, or equation rather than an agent's summary as though it were the source.
- Appraise sources for their intended role. Record the reasons for fit, authority, support, currency, and clarity. Prestige, peer review, citations, search rank, and visual polish are insufficient guarantees.
- Preserve assumptions, qualification, contrary evidence, and limits of generalization. Identify a result reported by one study as such. Seek independent support when the claim's uncertainty warrants it; copies of the same finding do not provide it.
- Label agent-proposed prerequisites, examples, and simplifications as pedagogical inferences or inventions. Do not attribute them to a source that did not state them.
- Use local notes first for already-inspected content. Reopen the relevant original for new details, ambiguous extraction, exact wording, contradictions, or freshness checks. A cache preserves a version; it does not prove that version remains current.

**Check:** Follow a representative central claim from the lesson or graph back to its source ID, inspected passage, and recorded version. Confirm that the passage supports the claim with the stated qualifications. Leave unsupported dependencies or content explicitly unresolved.
