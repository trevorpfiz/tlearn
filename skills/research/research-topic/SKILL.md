# Research a learning topic

Build a small, reusable source packet that can support prerequisite mapping and accurate lesson content. Deliver `sources.md`, selected source notes, and useful optional originals in the learner's project.

## Frame and reuse

Start from the topic, question, paper, or prerequisite gap. State the bounded outcome and the questions that need sources. Assume everyday language and basic arithmetic; domain foundations need teaching materials. Do not ask the learner to inventory their knowledge.

Read an existing `sources.md` first, then only the relevant source notes. Reuse inspected passages when they cover the question and version. Search again for uncovered concepts, unresolved claims, stale methods, or changed source status. Keep prerequisite suggestions separate from the source's own statements.

## Discover and select

Choose sources by their job. Expert textbooks, maintained courses, and reference works can teach stable foundations. Reviews help orient a field and locate studies. Inspect original research for specific empirical or novel claims, and official versioned documentation for software behavior. A user-supplied paper anchors the objective but still needs appraisal and supporting foundations.

Search the needed concepts and synonyms through suitable scholarly indexes, institutional resources, or official documentation. Record the useful queries and search date. Screen titles and abstracts before reading deeply; follow references when they fill a concrete gap. Avoid collecting a library simply because more results exist.

Read [source quality](references/source-quality.md) when selecting candidates. Use its screening gates and separate judgments for fit, authority, support, currency, and clarity. Record reasons and passage locations, not a composite score. Verify unfamiliar creators through independent information and trace derivative claims to their origin. For disputed, surprising, or important uncertain claims, seek independent evidence and record disagreement; repeated reports of one study are not independent confirmation.

Apply [source integrity](../principle-source-integrity/SKILL.md). Search snippets and uninspected references are leads, not verified support. Partial access limits what can be taught from a source; find another accessible version or retain the gap. For a paper, consult [reading papers](references/reading-papers.md), especially when extracting methods, figures, limitations, or computational resources.

## Inspect and preserve

Read [the source format](references/source-format.md) when saving the packet. Always retain source identity, version or retrieval date, canonical link, inspected scope, role, selection rationale, and concise claim-linked notes. Preserve definitions, mechanisms, relevant examples, assumptions, and uncertainties with original passage locators. Separate source findings, author interpretation, and pedagogical inference.

Use a hybrid storage policy: notes are the working context; originals are available evidence. Cache selected PDFs or web snapshots when permitted and useful for reuse, figures, fragile access, or version stability. Do not download every candidate. Reuse a supplied local file without duplicating it unless portability needs a copy. Store cached originals separately from extraction and agent-written notes; record their identity and checksum. A saved file need not be loaded in full.

Inspect critical tables, diagrams, and equations in their original presentation if extraction could change their meaning. Record access and extraction problems. Use current repository-supported retrieval services for automated downloads; record reuse terms before distributing source copies.

## Close the current pass

Connect each research question or known prerequisite to inspected supporting passages, or mark a partial answer or gap. Choose the smallest set that covers the current outcome with credible support and usable teaching material. Stop when remaining candidates add no needed coverage, a scope boundary is reached, or an access problem requires a different route. Do not claim exhaustive coverage.

Verify source IDs and paths, passage fidelity, selection reasons, and unresolved disagreements. Hand off `sources.md` and the gaps for knowledge mapping. When mapping discovers more required prerequisites, extend this packet rather than repeat the whole search.
