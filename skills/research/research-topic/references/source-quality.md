# Source quality and selection

Read when comparing candidates or checking whether a source can support a prerequisite or lesson claim. This is a repeatable tlearn appraisal method, not a validated quality scale. Agent judgment remains necessary, but its evidence and reasons must be inspectable.

## Match the source to its job

| Needed job | Good starting sources | Inspect for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teach a stable foundation | Expert textbook, university course, disciplinary reference | Accurate definitions, worked examples, prerequisite burden, and qualifications |
| Explain a field or contested question | Scholarly review or synthesis plus relevant original work | Search or selection scope, competing explanations, and the origins of central claims |
| Support a particular empirical result | Original study and relevant independent evidence | Design, observations, analyses, uncertainty, and the conclusion actually supported |
| Explain a computational method | Original methods paper, maintained method documentation, suitable tutorial | Assumptions, data requirements, validation, simplifications, and implementation details |
| Teach current software behavior | Official documentation for the relevant release | Version match, examples, deprecations, and reproducible behavior |

Primary research is often harder to teach from than a textbook. Clear teaching material does not by itself validate a novel empirical claim. One source can fill several roles, but appraise it for each role it will serve. Expert essays can provide useful explanations or practitioner perspectives; preserve that evidence type.

## Screen before deep reading

For a promising candidate, answer these gates with a short reason:

1. **Relevant:** which current question or capability could it support?
2. **Identifiable:** can the creator, original venue, and version be established? Trace a repost to its origin.
3. **Inspectable:** can the relevant passage be read? Title or snippet discovery is insufficient. Abstract-only access supports only what the abstract actually states.
4. **Usable status:** is the material appropriate to use now? Check known corrections, retractions, version changes, and the effect of any notice on the proposed claim. A correction is not an automatic rejection; use and cite the corrected information.

Keep promising uninspected material as `candidate`. Use `included` for inspected sources selected for specified roles, `context` for material useful only for orientation, and `excluded` for a considered source not used, with the reason. Inspection scope remains explicit even for included sources. Do not assign a favorable judgment to missing information.

## Record a profile, not a total

For each included source, record `strong`, `adequate`, `weak`, or `unknown` on each dimension, with a brief evidence-based reason. The profile is scoped to the selected role and passages, not every claim in the document.

- **Strong:** direct, inspectable evidence satisfies the dimension for the intended role, with no material gap identified.
- **Adequate:** supports the bounded use with limitations that are explicit and do not invalidate that use.
- **Weak:** a material mismatch, concern, or unmet condition undermines the intended use.
- **Unknown:** the available information does not permit a judgment. Name what would resolve it.

| Dimension | Question | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit | Does it address the target question at the needed depth? | Exact section and scope; what remains uncovered |
| Authority | Is its expertise or responsibility appropriate for this role? | Relevant author expertise, editorial process, or maintainer responsibility, checked beyond self-description when uncertain |
| Support | Do the inspected evidence and reasoning justify the proposed claim? | Derivation, data and methods, references, uncertainty, known limitations |
| Currency | Is this edition, result, or release suitable now? | Source version, publication/update date, corrections, subsequent evidence, and date of status check |
| Clarity | Can it help teach the capability from the assumed baseline? | Definitions, examples, representations, explicit assumptions, and required background |

An older foundational explanation can be appropriate; a recently posted tutorial can be wrong. A methods paper with weak clarity can still be strong evidence, paired with a better teaching resource. Weak or unknown support for a central claim cannot be compensated by a famous author or excellent diagrams. Check a missing condition, change the source's role, find better evidence, or keep the claim unresolved.

Use claim-level corroboration for important uncertainties. Check whether supposedly independent sources share the same study, dataset, or author argument. Preserve disagreement rather than choose by vote. For established definitions, do not manufacture a requirement for two citations to every sentence.

## Select a complementary set and stop

Select for coverage, reliable support, useful explanation, and little redundancy. A further source should fill a gap, improve teaching clarity, resolve disagreement, or add independent evidence where needed. Favor accessible equivalents after considering fitness; free availability alone is not a quality judgment. Record important excluded alternatives or access limitations, not every search hit.

In `sources.md`, mark each research question or known prerequisite as `supported`, `partial`, or `gap`, naming the supporting passages and remaining need. Stop the current pass when its bounded questions are adequately supported or its limits are explicit. Mapping may reopen the search for newly identified prerequisites.

## Grounding and limits

[ACRL](https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework) treats authority as contextual. [Civic Online Reasoning](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/about/) motivates checking the creator, evidence, and other sources rather than trusting a site's presentation. These guide source investigation; they do not validate scientific results.

[CASP](https://casp-uk.net/casp-tools-checklists/) offers study-specific appraisal questions. Its [diagnostic checklist guidance](https://casp-uk.net/checklists-archive/casp-diagnostic-studies-checklist-fillable.pdf) records reasons and does not recommend a scoring system. Borrow that transparent approach; use an actual study-specific tool when its scope fits, rather than grading textbooks or algorithms as clinical trials.

[Cochrane chapter 4](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04) informs explicit selection criteria, search records, and linking multiple reports of one study. tlearn narrows those practices to a learning objective. Our ordinary search is not evidence of systematic-review completeness.
