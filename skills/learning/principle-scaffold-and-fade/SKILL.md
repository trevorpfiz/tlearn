# Scaffold and fade

Supply enough support for a useful attempt, then remove support so the learner performs the reasoning.

**Why:** A novice can get stuck coordinating several unfamiliar steps. Permanent worked solutions can also hide dependence on assistance.

**Apply:**

- Show a short worked example with named subgoals and a brief reason for important choices. Keep the relevant representation and explanation together.
- Follow with a different instance that leaves a meaningful step to complete; then ask for a fresh solution without the worked steps. Fade hints, method labels, and procedural cues as well as arithmetic steps.
- Introduce new difficulties gradually. Keep established components active instead of changing every representation and demand at once.
- Ask a brief “why does this step apply?” where it helps distinguish principled reasoning from copying. Direct instruction and explanation prompts can coexist.
- Make optional hints progressively specific: orient attention, recall the relevant concept, then show a step. Revealing help changes the evidence supplied by that attempt.
- For an error, identify the plausible component gap, give specific feedback, link the ordinary earlier section, and offer a fresh retry. Avoid asserting a diagnosis from one answer. If problems recur, inspect the prompt, key, and prerequisite route too.

**Check:** Can the learner progress from a modeled task to a materially similar task with less help, and finally to an independent one? Is there a usable repair route without restarting the whole lesson?

**Sources:** [The Math Academy Way](https://www.justinmath.com/books/), micro-scaffolding and targeted remediation; [Atkinson, Renkl, and Merrill (2003)](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/transitioning-from-studying-examples-to-solving-problems-effects-/).
