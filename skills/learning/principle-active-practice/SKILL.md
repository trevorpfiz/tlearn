# Active practice

Give information to enable an action, then ask the learner to perform it.

**Why:** Reading or watching can introduce an idea. An attempt exposes whether the learner can retrieve, explain, predict, or use it. Interface activity alone supplies little evidence of that capability.

**Apply:**

- Keep instruction close to the task it enables. Introduce only the concepts and notation needed for the next small attempt; retain enough explanation to make the task fair.
- Match the response to the goal: recall a relation, predict a change, explain a mechanism, interpret a result, or execute a step. Use concise prompts; an essay is rarely needed for a small capability.
- Pair meaningful visuals with a prediction before reveal, comparison, explanation, or error correction. Clicking, dragging, and watching an animation are not sufficient checks by themselves.
- Give feedback after an attempt. Explain the relevant distinction and offer a fresh attempt; do not reveal the answer through nearby labels or prefilled controls.
- Use selection questions when discriminating alternatives is the goal. Include generated responses or execution where recognition alone would miss the capability.

**Check:** For each taught capability, identify what the learner produces and what that response lets you assess. If removing the response leaves the lesson unchanged, redesign the activity.

**Sources:** Skycak's [biology learning account](https://www.justinmath.com/q-and-a-4/) and [Chi and Wylie's ICAP framework](https://education.asu.edu/sites/default/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf). See [evidence and limits](../design-learning/references/learning-evidence.md#active-practice).
