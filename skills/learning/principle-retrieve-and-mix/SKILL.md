# Retrieve and mix

Ask for recall before revealing; mix established capabilities when choosing between them is part of the goal.

**Why:** Familiarity with an explanation can conceal weak recall. A run of identically labeled problems can supply the method instead of teaching the learner to choose it.

**Apply:**

- After initial instruction, ask the learner to retrieve a fact, relation, explanation, or procedure before reopening the answer. Allow an explicit reveal when needed; follow it with a fresh attempt.
- Give unfamiliar skills some similar initial practice. Introduce easily confused concepts clearly before mixing or contrasting them.
- Revisit earlier components after intervening material and inside later application tasks. Keep these revisits relevant to the narrow objective.
- Mix tasks for which method selection or conceptual discrimination matters. Remove headings and cues that announce the answer or procedure, while retaining the information needed to solve the task.
- Vary consequential features: direction of change, representation, data conditions, or applicable assumptions. Random ordering and cosmetic number changes alone do not guarantee useful variation.
- Correct retrieval errors promptly. Avoid repeatedly rehearsing a wrong rule.

**Check:** Does a mixed task require deciding what applies? Can a later response be attempted without the explanation visible? Describe these as within-tool revisits; repeated practice in one session does not establish retention across days.

**Sources:** [Karpicke and Blunt (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/) and Skycak on [interleaving](https://www.justinmath.com/cognitive-science-of-learning-interleaving/). See [evidence and limits](../design-learning/references/learning-evidence.md#retrieve-and-mix).
